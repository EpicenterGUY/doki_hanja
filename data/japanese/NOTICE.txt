HANJA LAB — Japanese kanji / vocabulary data notice

Bundled kanji files
===================

1) joyo.tsv
- Jōyō kanji inventory: 2,136 characters.
- Includes school grade, radical, stroke count and old-form information.

2) hyogai.txt + hyogai-meta.json
- The original core is derived from the 2000 Hyōgai Kanji Jitai-hyō (表外漢字字体表) inventory supplied by:
  https://github.com/xiaolanchong/kanji_dashboard
  Source files: data/joyo.txt, data/hyogai.txt
  That repository declares ISC license in package.json.
- The 2000 table predates the 2010 Jōyō revision. Hanja Lab removes current Jōyō characters from the core.
- Parenthesized characters in the original hyogai source are alternate/simplified glyph forms and remain metadata rather than separate base study characters.
- Hanja Lab additionally exposes non-Jōyō characters in the JIS X 0208 Level 1 and Level 2 repertoires as separate expansion groups.
- IMPORTANT: JIS Level 1 / Level 2 are character-encoding classifications. They are not official usage recommendations, school grades, or frequency rankings.
- Current legacy study-base counts in hyogai.txt:
  · core: 875
  · JIS Level 1 extra: 382
  · JIS Level 2 extra: 2,985
  · legacy base study characters: 4,242
  · legacy unique non-Jōyō glyphs including retained variants: 4,261
- Hanja Lab 2.49.0 additionally loads data/japanese/hyogai-jis3-extra.json:
  · JIS X 0213 Level 3 ultra-rare supplement: 96 characters
  · runtime non-Jōyō study/reference total: 4,338 characters
  · these 96 characters stay in the rare/non-practical pool and never enter the default practical pool.
- The JIS3 supplement is curated from the JIS X 0213 Level 3 table published by みんなの漢字辞典:
  https://minkan.co.jp/others/level3/
  Hanja Lab stores the character, reading, stroke count and JIS plane-row-cell code needed for local study/search.

The official Japanese government reference for the core concept is the Agency for Cultural Affairs (文化庁) 表外漢字字体表.

Additional bundled reading data
===============================
- mext-onkun-2017.json: official Jōyō kanji on/kun readings and school-stage allocation extracted from Japan MEXT's
  "音訓の小・中・高等学校段階別割り振り表（平成29年3月）".

Source repository:
https://github.com/make1s/diglabo-kanji-data

Source file:
- data/mext-onkun-2017.json

That repository states CC BY-SA 4.0 for the dataset and includes the original MEXT source metadata inside the JSON.
Hanja Lab preserves the source metadata and uses this file to distinguish readings listed in the official Jōyō table from additional readings.

Vocabulary expansion
====================
- words.json contains Hanja Lab's Korean learning meanings/examples plus expanded Japanese vocabulary.
- Hanja Lab 2.49.0 also loads rare-words-extra.json, a 293-entry rare/non-practical supplement. The supplement contains Hanja Lab-authored Korean glosses, contextual Japanese examples and Korean translations; every example preserves the exact target kanji spelling.
- Part of the 2026-10-02 Hyōgai expansion cross-references the Jonathan Waller JLPT vocabulary lists as distributed with JMdict sequence IDs by:
  https://github.com/stephenmk/yomitan-jlpt-vocab
  and mirrored/consumed by:
  https://github.com/coolmule0/JLPT-N5-N1-Japanese-Vocabulary-Anki
- The source documentation states CC BY-SA 4.0 for the JLPT list data.
- Hanja Lab stores its own Korean glosses and learning sentences; it does not expose the English Waller definitions in the UI.
- The 2026-10-06 rare/non-practical vocabulary expansion also curates Japanese difficult-kanji word/readings from https://kanjitisiki.com/nandoku/ and four-character idiom readings from https://kanjitisiki.com/yoji/ . It also includes selected historical country-name ateji from the site's country-name list. Hanja Lab applies its own rare-character filter and supplies its own Korean glosses, contextual example sentences, translations and study metadata.
- JLPT labels are study-list metadata, not an official JLPT vocabulary specification published by the Japan Foundation/Japan Educational Exchanges and Services.


Staged Hyogai vocabulary (Hanja Lab)
====================================
- As of Hanja Lab 2.49.0, the runtime Hyogai vocabulary contains 759 practical-scope words and 759 rare/non-practical-scope words (1,518 total).
- The word-writing UI groups them into four Hanja Lab study stages: 입문 (entry), 실용 (practical), 고급 (advanced), 심화 (deep).
- Rare/non-practical words are available in their own collection for both word-writing and word-reading practice; they do not mix into the default practical Hyogai pool.
- These four stages are learning buckets, not an official Japanese government, JIS, or JLPT usage ranking.
- Explicitly curated 2.43.0 additions carry hyogaiStage metadata; older entries are mapped at runtime using available JLPT metadata and the character's Hanja Lab Hyogai group.


Practical Hyogai default (Hanja Lab 2.49.0)
===========================================
- The runtime non-Joyo inventory is 4,338 characters for reference/search: the previous 4,242-character inventory plus 96 JIS X 0213 Level 3 ultra-rare characters.
- Default Hyogai character study remains limited to the 661-character practical pool used by the practical-scope vocabulary/example entries.
- Breakdown of the 661 practical characters:
  · core: 379
  · JIS Level 1 extra: 85
  · JIS Level 2 extra: 197
- The remaining 3,677 characters remain a separate rare/non-practical character collection rather than being mixed into the default 661-character practical pool.
- Curated words containing those rare characters can be studied separately through the rare word-writing and word-reading collection.
- This is a Hanja Lab study-priority rule, not an official Japanese government,
  JIS or JLPT classification.


JLPT N5-N1 learning classification (Hanja Lab 2.44.0)
=====================================================
- Source: OpenJLPT by evanclan
  https://github.com/evanclan/OpenJLPT
- OpenJLPT normalizes and cross-checks the Jonathan Waller community JLPT lists.
- License: CC BY-SA 4.0. Hanja Lab stores only the level metadata needed by
  data/japanese/jlpt-levels.json and attributes the source here.
- JLPT has not published an official fixed kanji/vocabulary list since 2010.
  Therefore N5-N1 labels in Hanja Lab are learning approximations, not an
  official allocation published by the Japan Foundation or JEES.
- Joyo kanji: 2,136 characters are mapped to N5-N1. Four Joyo characters not
  present in the upstream community kanji list (叱, 填, 剥, 頬) are placed in
  the N1 learning bucket as a conservative Hanja Lab fallback.
- Joyo vocabulary: 1,542 bundled words are classified. 1,060 match OpenJLPT
  by exact word+reading, 8 use a unique word-form match, and 474 fall back to
  the hardest JLPT level among the word's mapped kanji.
- Hyogai practical 661 characters and rare/non-practical 3,677 characters keep
  the existing Hanja Lab classification and are not folded into the Joyo JLPT
  buckets.


KANJIDIC2 full reading index (Hanja Lab 2.46.0)
====================================================
- data/japanese/kanjidic-readings.json adds a bundled on-yomi / kun-yomi index for
  the Hanja Lab Joyo + Hyogai primary-character inventory.
- Source data: KANJIDIC2 from the Electronic Dictionary Research and Development
  Group (EDRDG), transformed by:
  https://github.com/jkindrix/japanese-language-data
  Source file: data/core/kanji.json
- The source dataset states CC-BY-SA 4.0 distribution and includes the EDRDG
  KANJIDIC2 attribution/license notice.
- Hanja Lab bundles only character -> on/kun reading arrays needed for the local
  reading index. Name-only nanori readings and dictionary-reference payloads are
  intentionally excluded from this derived file.
- kanjidic-readings.json covers 6,371 Hanja Lab primary characters with available
  readings (9,062 on-reading entries and 9,650 kun-reading entries in the source
  snapshot used for this build).
- Hanja Lab 2.49.0 merges the 96 locally curated JIS3 supplement readings at
  runtime, bringing the local reading-key set to 6,467 characters for this build.
- MEXT mext-onkun-2017.json remains the authority for identifying which Joyo
  readings are official Joyo-table readings. KANJIDIC2 supplements non-table
  readings and supplies Hyogai / rare readings.
- In the UI, the global reading index may show the same character under every
  applicable reading. This is intentional and fixes the old primary-reading-only
  grouping behavior.
